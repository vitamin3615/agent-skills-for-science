# Scientific Manuscript Editing

Improve scientific communication without inventing evidence. Match the edit to the user's requested scope rather than imposing a fixed intensity or journal style.

## Information integrity

Read [the integrity protocol](references/integrity-protocol.md) before substantive editing.

- Never invent or infer missing numbers, sample sizes, methods, statistics, citations, outcomes, or mechanistic details.
- Preserve the direction, magnitude, uncertainty, and evidentiary status of claims.
- Do not silently change technical meaning. Flag a suspected error or inconsistency and propose a correction separately.
- Keep citation support attached to the proposition it supports. Do not imply that a citation verifies a stronger claim than the supplied source establishes.
- When the user requests shortening or restructuring, facts may be removed or moved intentionally; identify any deletion that could materially change interpretation.
- Mark placeholders for information the manuscript needs instead of fabricating it.

## Choose the editing scope

Infer scope from the request and material. Ask only when the difference would be consequential.

- **Copy edit:** grammar, syntax, concision, terminology, and flow.
- **Scientific edit:** claim calibration, logical sequence, quantitative precision, and section function.
- **Structural edit:** reorganize, merge, shorten, or rebuild sections around the supplied evidence.
- **Evaluation:** diagnose strengths and problems without rewriting unless requested.
- **Construction:** draft a section from supplied findings; use explicit placeholders for absent facts.

Do not classify the author as human, AI, or ESL. Edit observable prose problems directly.

## Editing approach

Determine the section's job, central message, and available evidence. Revise at the level needed to make the logic clear. Check that:

- each claim is supported at the stated strength;
- comparisons identify groups, direction, magnitude, and uncertainty when those data are supplied;
- when a consequential quantitative claim lacks an adjusted effect estimate, uncertainty interval, or supporting analysis, the editorial query names those missing elements rather than calculating or implying them;
- Results report observations without unsupported mechanism or causal language;
- Discussion distinguishes findings, interpretation, limitations, and implications;
- Introduction narrows from the established problem to the specific unresolved question;
- Methods remain reproducible and do not trade technical precision for elegance;
- terminology, abbreviations, tense, and citation placement remain consistent.

Journal requirements are temporally unstable. If a target journal or article type matters, verify its current author instructions rather than treating local style examples as rules.

## Conditional references

The core instructions are usually sufficient. For a long manuscript, read the
[long-document protocol](references/meta-long-document-protocol.md); for a requested
change log, read the [tracked-changes template](references/track-changes-template.md).
Use [word-level precision](references/word-level-precision.md) or
[prose quality](references/prose-quality-ai-detection.md) only for a diagnosed prose
problem. Legacy section templates are examples, not requirements; prefer the actual
manuscript, target journal's current instructions, and the author's rhetorical goal.

## Delivery

Follow the requested format. By default, provide the revised text and a short note about substantive changes or unresolved factual questions. Do not add an evaluation rubric, alternatives, or a line-by-line diff unless useful or requested.
