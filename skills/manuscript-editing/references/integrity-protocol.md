# Scientific Editing Integrity Protocol

Use this protocol whenever editing changes scientific prose, structure, or emphasis.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- Do not invent data, sample sizes, statistical results, methods, citations, mechanisms, limitations, or outcomes.
- Preserve numerical values, units, comparison direction, uncertainty, and qualification unless the user supplies a correction.
- Do not strengthen association into causation, possibility into demonstration, or exploratory evidence into confirmation.
- Keep citations attached to the claims they support. Do not assign a citation to a new claim without checking the source.
- Preserve discipline-specific terms when a simpler substitute would change meaning.
- Flag internal inconsistencies, suspected factual errors, and unsupported claims instead of silently resolving them.

## Allowed changes

The user's requested scope controls what can change.

- Copy editing may alter grammar, syntax, order within a sentence, and redundant wording.
- Scientific editing may recalibrate claims and reorganize paragraphs while preserving the supplied evidence.
- Structural editing may move, merge, condense, or delete material when requested.
- Construction may synthesize only the information supplied and should mark missing essentials with explicit placeholders.

Information preservation does not mean every sentence must remain. When shortening or restructuring, preserve what is necessary for the requested message and identify omissions that could materially affect interpretation.

## Handling missing information

Do not replace vague source text with invented precision. Either retain a calibrated formulation or insert a visible placeholder, for example:

- [report sample size]
- [specify statistical model and uncertainty interval]
- [citation needed]
- [clarify whether this analysis was prespecified]

Questions are preferable when the missing fact blocks a defensible edit; otherwise use a placeholder and continue.

## Delivery check

Before returning an edit, compare it with the source for:

- changed numbers, groups, directions, or endpoints;
- added causal or mechanistic meaning;
- altered uncertainty or limitations;
- orphaned or reassigned citations;
- substantive deletions not requested or disclosed.

When proposing a scientific correction, distinguish the edited text from the recommendation so the user can accept it deliberately.
