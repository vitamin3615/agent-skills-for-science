# Common Manuscript Editing Mistakes

Use these contrasts to catch edits that change scientific meaning. The examples deliberately avoid supplying facts that were absent from the source.

## Inventing precision

Source:
> Treatment reduced tumor burden.

Unsafe:
> Treatment reduced tumor burden by 45% (n = 6; P < 0.05).

The percentage, sample size, and test result are fabricated. Preserve the sentence and add a separate note such as: `[Please provide the effect estimate, uncertainty, sample size, and analysis if available.]`

## Strengthening a claim

Source:
> The results suggest that NUPR1 is associated with iron availability.

Unsafe:
> NUPR1 controls iron availability.

Association does not establish direction or causation. A safe clarity edit is: `The results suggest an association between NUPR1 and iron availability.`

## Adding an unstated mechanism or method

Source:
> We measured protein abundance by immunoblotting.

Unsafe additions include a vendor, catalog number, dilution, incubation condition, normalization method, or statistical test not supplied by the author. Request each material detail in an editorial note; do not guess it.

## Deleting a null result or limitation

Source:
> RP did not alter neuronal survival but increased astrocyte survival.

Unsafe:
> RP increased astrocyte survival.

The edit removes a result that defines the intervention's scope. Preserve null findings, adverse findings, caveats, and boundary conditions unless the author explicitly asks to remove them and the removal does not mislead.

## Forcing style

Do not automatically replace every passive construction, expand simple prose into jargon, compress all findings into one sentence, or force a stock journal voice. Edit only where the change improves clarity, coherence, precision, or compliance with an identified requirement.

## Reorganizing without traceability

Moving text can be appropriate, but do not silently drop content. When restructuring materially, provide a clean revision plus a short change summary; flag any passage whose intended meaning is uncertain.

## Final integrity check

Before returning an edit, compare it with the source and verify:

- every number, entity, method, citation, and mechanistic statement is source-supported;
- qualifiers, negation, comparison direction, and population scope are preserved;
- source-supported deletions or major relocations are disclosed;
- requests for missing information are comments or placeholders, not manuscript claims.
