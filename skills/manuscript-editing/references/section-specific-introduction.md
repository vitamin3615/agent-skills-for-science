# Introduction-Specific Editing Guidelines

**Purpose:** Establish biological context, review literature, identify knowledge gap, and introduce this study's approach.

**Target Length:** 3-5 paragraphs for CNS journals

**Structure:** Funnel approach—Broad → Specific → This Study

---

## Required Structure

### Paragraph 1: Biological/Medical Significance (Broad Context)

**Purpose:** Establish why this topic matters to science or medicine

**What to include:**
- Broad importance of the biological process/disease/system
- General significance to human health or fundamental biology
- Why this area deserves investigation

**Examples:**
- Disease significance: "Cancer is the second leading cause of death, with X million cases annually..."
- Biological process: "Stem cell maintenance is critical for tissue homeostasis and regeneration..."
- Fundamental question: "Understanding how cells sense and respond to metabolic stress is central to..."

**Scope:** Start BROAD, not narrow
- ✅ "Aging affects all tissues and is the primary risk factor for chronic disease"
- ❌ "SUV39H1 is a histone methyltransferase" (too specific for opening)

---

### Paragraphs 2-3: Current Knowledge and Limitations

**Purpose:** Review what's known and identify what remains unclear

**What to include:**
- Summary of key prior findings (with citations)
- Technical/biological challenges in the field
- Conflicting results or controversies
- Explicit statement of what remains unknown

**Literature integration:**
- ✅ Comprehensive citations (10-30 references typical for CNS papers)
- ✅ Synthesize findings, don't just list papers
- ✅ Organize by concept/theme, not chronologically

**Citation density:**
- High-impact claims: Multiple citations
- Established facts: 1-2 citations
- Controversial points: Represent different views

**Gap statement (CRITICAL):**
Must explicitly state what's unknown using phrases like:
- "However, whether X regulates Y remains unclear"
- "Nevertheless, the mechanism by which X affects Y is unknown"
- "Despite these advances, how X contributes to Y has not been tested"
- "Yet whether X is required in vivo remains to be determined"

**What makes a GOOD gap:**
- ✅ Specific and focused (not generic)
- ✅ Directly addressed by your study
- ✅ Represents genuine unknown, not just "we haven't done this yet"
- ✅ Positioned as important to the field

**What makes a WEAK gap:**
- ❌ Generic: "More research is needed"
- ❌ Already solved: "The role of X is unclear" (but literature already showed it)
- ❌ Trivial: "We don't know if this works in a different strain"
- ❌ Disconnected: Gap has nothing to do with your results

---

### Paragraph 4: Study Rationale and Approach

**Purpose:** Introduce THIS study and explain the experimental strategy

**Required elements:**

**1. Study introduction:**
- "Here we..." or "In this study, we..."
- "We now report..."
- "To address this, we..."

**2. Experimental system:**
- Model organism/system used
- Why this system is appropriate
- Any novel tools or approaches

**3. Strategy overview:**
- High-level view of experimental design
- Key methods/approaches
- Why this approach addresses the gap

**Example:**
> "Here, using physiologically aged mice and single-cell RNA sequencing, we investigated how aging affects alveolar type 2 (AT2) stem cells and their tumorigenic potential. We combined genetic lineage tracing with loss-of-function experiments to define the molecular mechanisms linking aging to cancer susceptibility."

**Tone:**
- Confident and direct
- Not defensive or apologetic
- Clear about innovation/advance

---

### Paragraph 5 (Optional): Preview of Major Findings/Implications

**Purpose:** Orient readers to key results (optional in Introductions)

**When to include:**
- Longer papers with complex findings
- When helpful to guide readers through results
- When CNS journal format includes preview

**When to omit:**
- Shorter papers
- If it feels redundant with abstract
- If it disrupts narrative flow into Results

---

## Editing Checklist for Introductions

**Structure:**
- [ ] Paragraph 1: Broad biological/medical significance
- [ ] Paragraphs 2-3: Literature review with comprehensive citations
- [ ] Explicit gap statement ("However, ... remains unclear/unknown")
- [ ] "Here we..." or equivalent study introduction
- [ ] Brief overview of experimental approach
- [ ] Logical flow: Broad → Specific → Gap → This Study

**Literature Integration:**
- [ ] 10-30 citations typical for CNS papers
- [ ] Citations comprehensive and current
- [ ] Multiple perspectives represented for controversial topics
- [ ] Synthesized by theme, not listed chronologically

**Voice and Style:**
- [ ] Progressive narrowing from broad to specific
- [ ] Smooth transitions between paragraphs
- [ ] Technical terms defined on first use
- [ ] No results discussed (save for Results section)
- [ ] Confident tone (not defensive)

**Gap Quality:**
- [ ] Gap explicitly stated (not implied)
- [ ] Gap specific and focused
- [ ] Gap directly addressed by Results
- [ ] Gap positioned as important to field

**Study Introduction:**
- [ ] Clear "Here we..." statement
- [ ] Experimental system specified
- [ ] Approach/strategy briefly described
- [ ] Connection to gap clear

## Common Introduction Issues and Fixes

**Issue 1: Too Narrow Opening**

**❌ WRONG:**
> "SUV39H1 is a histone methyltransferase that catalyzes H3K9 trimethylation."

**✅ CORRECT:**
> "Aging is associated with epigenetic alterations that affect gene expression and cellular function. Among these, histone methylation patterns change dramatically with age, with potential consequences for stem cell maintenance and disease susceptibility."

**Why better:** Starts with broad significance, then narrows to specific mechanism.

---

**Issue 2: No Clear Gap**

**❌ WRONG:**
> "Many studies have investigated aging. We studied aging in lung stem cells."

**✅ CORRECT:**
> "While aging's effects on tissue homeostasis are well-documented^1-5^, whether aging cell-intrinsically affects cancer susceptibility in stem cells remains unclear."

**Why better:** Explicit gap statement with "remains unclear."

---

**Issue 3: Missing "Here we..." Statement**

**❌ WRONG:**
> "This study examines aged mice. Single-cell RNA sequencing was performed."

**✅ CORRECT:**
> "Here, using physiologically aged mice, we demonstrate that aging cell-intrinsically suppresses lung cancer by degrading AT2 stem cell function."

**Why better:** Clear study introduction with "Here we..." and main finding stated.

---

**Issue 4: Poor Transition to Results**

**❌ WRONG:**
> "Iron is important for cells. We did experiments."

**✅ CORRECT:**
> "To determine whether iron availability regulates stem cell function during aging, we performed comprehensive iron metabolite profiling and functional rescue experiments."

**Why better:** Clear transition stating what was tested and how.

---

**Issue 5: Weak Literature Integration**

**❌ WRONG:**
> "Smith et al. showed X. Jones et al. showed Y. Brown et al. showed Z."

**✅ CORRECT:**
> "Multiple lines of evidence suggest that iron homeostasis changes with aging, including altered transferrin levels^12^, reduced iron uptake^13^, and increased ferritin expression^14,15^. However, the functional consequences for stem cells remain unclear."

**Why better:** Synthesizes findings thematically with multiple citations, then identifies gap.

## Transition Phrases for Introductions

**Opening paragraphs (establishing context):**
- "X is essential for..."
- "Understanding Y is critical because..."
- "Z represents a major challenge in..."

**Literature review (synthesizing knowledge):**
- "Multiple studies have shown..."
- "Accumulating evidence suggests..."
- "Recent work has revealed..."
- "Despite these advances..."

**Identifying gaps (signaling unknown):**
- "However, whether X affects Y remains unclear"
- "Nevertheless, the mechanism by which..."
- "Yet, the role of X in vivo has not been tested"
- "Despite this progress, how X regulates Y is unknown"

**Introducing study (signaling novelty):**
- "Here we show..."
- "Here, using X, we demonstrate..."
- "To address this, we..."
- "We now report..."

**Connecting paragraphs:**
- "Despite these insights..." (transition to gap)
- "To investigate this..." (transition to approach)
- "Given these observations..." (transition to rationale)

- [ ] Recent literature included

- [ ] Approach briefly described
- [ ] Rationale clear

## Example Introduction Paragraph

**BEFORE (Weak):**
> "Aging is important. Many people study aging. We looked at aging in mice. We found interesting results."

**Problems:**
- Generic opening
- No specific gap
- No literature citations
- No clear study introduction
- No experimental approach described

---

**AFTER (CNS-Quality):**
> "Aging is the primary risk factor for cancer, with incidence rates increasing exponentially after age 50^1,2^. However, aging is also associated with stem cell decline, which impairs tissue regeneration and cellular proliferation^3-6^. This creates a paradox: if aging reduces stem cell function, how does cancer incidence increase? In the lung, alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells serve as facultative stem cells that can both maintain homeostasis and serve as cells-of-origin for adenocarcinoma^7,8^. While several studies have shown that aging affects AT2 cell proliferative capacity^9,10^, whether aging-driven stem cell decline cell-intrinsically affects tumorigenesis has not been tested in physiologically aged animals. Here, using aged Kras^LSL-G12D/+^;Trp53^fl/fl^ mice, we demonstrate that aging suppresses lung cancer initiation and progression by degrading AT2 stem cell function through a NUPR1–lipocalin-2–iron insufficiency axis. Our findings reveal that aging's effects on cancer are cell-intrinsic and mechanistically distinct from systemic or microenvironmental changes."

**Improvements:**
- ✅ Broad significance (aging-cancer connection)
- ✅ Specific paradox/gap (aging ↓ stem cells, but ↑ cancer—how?)
- ✅ Comprehensive citations (10 references)
- ✅ Explicit gap ("has not been tested in physiologically aged animals")
- ✅ Clear "Here, using X, we demonstrate..."
- ✅ Specific experimental system (aged KP mice)
- ✅ Key finding stated (aging suppresses cancer via iron axis)
- ✅ Innovation highlighted (cell-intrinsic vs. systemic)
