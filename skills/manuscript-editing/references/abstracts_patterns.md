## Standard Structure (8 Parts, 150-250 words)

### 1. Opening Context (1-2 sentences)

**Purpose:** Establish broad biological/medical significance

**Patterns:**

- "[Process/System] is essential for [function]"
- "[Disease/Condition] is characterized by [feature]"
- "Aging is associated with..."
- "The maintenance of [process] involves..."

**Examples:**

- "Ageing is associated with a decline in the number and fitness of adult stem cells."
- "Sexual reproduction relies on meiotic chromosome pairing..."
- "Iron catalyzes the oxidation of lipids in biological membranes..."

### 2. Knowledge Gap (1 sentence)

**Purpose:** State what remains unknown

**Key phrases:**

- "However, [X] remains unclear/unknown/poorly understood"
- "Despite [progress], whether [Y] ... is unknown"
- "It is unclear whether/how..."
- "The mechanisms underlying [X] remain elusive"

**Examples:**

- "However, how this positive feedback is restricted to maintain H3K9me3 homeostasis remains largely unknown."
- "Whether this self-recognition mechanism also exists in astrocytes remains unknown."

### 3. Study Statement (1 sentence)

**Purpose:** Introduce what THIS study did

**Standard openings:**

- "Here we..."
- "We show that..."
- "We demonstrate that..."
- "We found that..."
- "We report..."
- "In this study, we..."

**Pattern:** "Here we [approach] to [goal/question]"

**Examples:**

- "Here we use physiologically aged autochthonous mice to demonstrate that ageing suppresses lung cancer..."
- "Here we show that γC3 acts autonomously to regulate astrocyte morphogenesis..."
- "We performed an unbiased genome-scale CRISPR-Cas9 screen and identified..."

### 4. Approach (1 sentence, optional)

**Purpose:** Briefly state key methods

**Patterns:**

- "Using [method], we..."
- "Through [approach], we identified..."
- "By [technique], we demonstrated..."

**Examples:**

- "Using genetic manipulation, we demonstrate that..."
- "Through systematic investigations, we demonstrate..."

### 5. Major Findings (2-3 sentences)

**Purpose:** Present specific, quantitative results

**Requirements:**

- Specific findings, not vague claims
- Quantitative where possible
- Key molecular players named
- Multiple findings in sequence

**Patterns:**

- "We found that [X] [did Y] [under condition Z]"
- "[Protein/gene] promotes/inhibits [process] by [mechanism]"
- "[Treatment] resulted in [quantified outcome]"

**Examples:**

- "This phenotype is underpinned by the ageing-associated induction of NUPR1 and lipocalin-2, which leads to functional iron insufficiency."
- "ASB7 is recruited to heterochromatin by HP1 and promotes SUV39H1 degradation."

### 6. Mechanistic Insight (1-2 sentences)

**Purpose:** Explain HOW/WHY findings occur

**Patterns:**

- "Mechanistically, [X] functions by..."
- "This occurs through..."
- "[Process] is mediated by..."
- "The mechanism involves..."

**Examples:**

- "Mechanistically, 7-DHC dictates ferroptosis surveillance by using the conjugated diene to exert its anti-phospholipid autoxidation function."
- "This mechanism ensures effective H3K9me3 restoration during the cell cycle."

### 7. Validation (1 sentence, optional)

**Purpose:** Note in vivo confirmation or multi-method validation

**Patterns:**

- "In vivo, [finding] was confirmed by..."
- "These findings were validated using..."
- "Genetic inactivation of [X] demonstrated..."

**Examples:**

- "Genetic inactivation of the NUPR1-lipocalin-2 axis rescues stemness and promotes tumorigenesis."
- "In mouse tumour models, [drug] reduced..."

### 8. Impact/Implications (1 sentence)

**Purpose:** State broader significance

**Patterns:**

- "These findings have implications for..."
- "Our results suggest that..."
- "This study establishes..."
- "These discoveries may enable..."

**Examples:**

- "These findings have implications for therapeutic modulation of cellular iron homeostasis in regenerative medicine and cancer prevention."
- "Thus, NanoFLUID represents an innovative bioelectronic platform for targeted delivery..."

## Key Quality Markers

✅ **Length:** 150-250 words (strictly enforced)
✅ **Voice:** Active ("We found..." not "It was found...")
✅ **Specificity:** Numbers, proteins, pathways named
✅ **No citations:** Abstracts standalone without references
✅ **Declarative:** Strong statements (appropriately hedged only when data-limited)
✅ **Progressive:** Each sentence advances the narrative
✅ **Technical terms:** Defined or avoided
✅ **Quantitative:** Fold-changes, percentages where relevant

## Common Patterns by Field

### Cell Biology

- Opening: "[Cellular process] is essential for [function]..."
- Gap: "However, the mechanisms underlying [X] remain unclear..."
- Findings: "[Protein] regulates [process] through [mechanism]..."

### Cancer Biology

- Opening: "[Cancer type] is characterized by [features]..."
- Gap: "Whether [X] affects [Y] in [context] is unknown..."
- Findings: "We demonstrate that [intervention] suppresses [outcome] by [mechanism]..."

### Immunology

- Opening: "[Immune process] requires [component]..."
- Gap: "How [X] is regulated during [condition] remains elusive..."
- Findings: "[Factor] activates [pathway] leading to [outcome]..."

### Molecular Biology

- Opening: "[Molecular process] involves [machinery]..."
- Gap: "The mechanistic basis for [phenomenon] remains unclear..."
- Findings: "We reveal that [factor] [action] through [mechanism]..."

---

## Transition Flow

**Typical flow pattern:**

1. Context → 2. "However, ..." (gap) → 3. "Here we..." (study) → 4-6. Findings + mechanism → 7-8. Impact

**Logical connectors:**

- Context to gap: "However, Despite, Although, Yet"
- Gap to study: "Here we, To address this, We sought to"
- Finding to finding: "Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore"
- Finding to mechanism: "Mechanistically, This occurs through"
- Findings to impact: "These findings, Together, Our results"

## Examples by Quality Level

### Excellent (CNS-level)

"Ageing is associated with a decline in the number and fitness of adult stem cells. Ageing-associated loss of stemness is posited to suppress tumorigenesis, but this hypothesis has not been tested in vivo. Here we use physiologically aged mice to demonstrate that ageing suppresses lung cancer initiation and progression by degrading stemness of alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells. This phenotype is underpinned by ageing-associated induction of NUPR1 and lipocalin-2, which leads to functional iron insufficiency in aged cells. Genetic inactivation of the NUPR1–lipocalin-2 axis rescues stemness and promotes tumorigenesis in aged AT2 cells. These findings have implications for therapeutic modulation of cellular iron homeostasis in regenerative medicine and cancer prevention."

**Why it's excellent:**
✓ Clear context and gap
✓ Explicit "Here we..." statement
✓ Specific molecular players (NUPR1, lipocalin-2)
✓ Mechanistic depth (functional iron insufficiency)
✓ Genetic validation stated
✓ Clear implications

### Weak (Below CNS standards)

"Aging affects stem cells in various ways. We studied aging in mice. We found that old mice had different stem cell properties. Some proteins were changed. This might be important for understanding aging."

**Problems:**
✗ Vague context
✗ No clear research gap
✗ Weak study statement
✗ No specific findings or mechanisms
✗ No quantitative data
✗ Generic impact statement
