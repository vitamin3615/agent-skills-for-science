# Discussion-Specific Editing Guidelines

**Purpose:** Interpret findings, position against literature, acknowledge limitations, and identify implications.

**Target Length:** 4-6 paragraphs for CNS journals

**Key principle:** Discussion = Interpretation, not repetition of Results.

---

## Required Structure

### Paragraph 1: Summary of Major Findings

**Purpose:** Restate key results in context of original question

**What to include:**
- 1-2 sentences summarizing main discoveries
- Connection to research gap from Introduction
- Framing in biological/medical context

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Don't copy-paste Results sentences
- ❌ Don't include new data
- ❌ Don't repeat statistics (reference them, don't restate)

**Example:**
> "Our findings demonstrate that aging cell-intrinsically suppresses lung cancer initiation and progression by degrading AT2 stem cell function. This suppression is mediated by an aging-induced NUPR1–lipocalin-2 axis that creates functional iron insufficiency, limiting proliferative capacity."

**Note:** This synthesizes Results (from multiple paragraphs) into a cohesive summary.

---

### Paragraphs 2-3: Mechanistic Interpretation and Literature Positioning

**Purpose:** Explain HOW/WHY findings occur and position vs. prior work

**What to include:**

**1. Mechanistic interpretation:**
- Biological explanation of findings
- How molecular events connect to phenotypes
- Why this mechanism makes biological sense

**Example:**
> "Iron is required for the activity of ribonucleotide reductase and other enzymes essential for DNA synthesis and cell proliferation^15^. By sequestering iron through lipocalin-2 induction, aging cells may limit the substrate availability necessary for rapid cell division, thereby suppressing tumor growth."

**2. Literature positioning (CRITICAL):**
- How findings relate to prior work
- Similarities with previous studies
- Differences from existing models
- Resolution of contradictions

**Must include citations:**
- ✅ "This finding extends prior work showing X^12,13^"
- ✅ "Our results contrast with studies in Y system^14^, suggesting..."
- ✅ "This mechanism is consistent with observations in Z^15-17^"

**Tone for contradictions:**
- Professional, not defensive
- Propose explanations for discrepancies
- Acknowledge alternative interpretations

**Example:**
> "Our findings contrast with epidemiological data showing increased cancer incidence with age in humans^12,13^. This apparent discrepancy may reflect differences between cell-intrinsic aging effects (suppressive, as we show) and systemic/microenvironmental changes (potentially permissive) that accumulate over decades in humans^14^."

---

### Paragraph 4: Limitations and Caveats

**Purpose:** Honestly acknowledge constraints and alternative interpretations

**CRITICAL:** CNS papers are strong BECAUSE they include limitations, not despite them

**What to include:**

**1. Model system limitations:**
- Specific model used (e.g., Kras-driven lung cancer)
- May not generalize to other contexts
- Specific age range examined

**Example:**
> "Our study focused on a Kras-driven lung adenocarcinoma model in mice aged 18-24 months. Whether similar iron insufficiency mechanisms operate in other oncogenic contexts (e.g., EGFR-driven, TP53-mutant) or tissue types remains to be determined."

**2. Technical constraints:**
- Methods unable to address certain questions
- Temporal resolution limits
- Causality vs. correlation caveats

**3. Alternative interpretations:**
- Other possible explanations for findings
- What remains unclear
- Experiments that would distinguish between models

**Tone:**
- Honest and direct
- Not defensive or apologetic
- Shows sophisticated scientific thinking

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Don't hide limitations
- ❌ Don't dismiss them as "minor"
- ❌ Don't over-apologize

---

### Paragraph 5: Broader Implications

**Purpose:** State impact on field and potential applications

**What to include:**

**1. Field-level impact:**
- How findings change understanding
- New framework or model
- Paradigm shifts

**Example:**
> "These findings challenge the assumption that aging uniformly promotes cancer. Instead, our work reveals that aging's effects are context-dependent, with cell-intrinsic changes potentially suppressing tumorigenesis even as systemic changes may be permissive."

**2. Therapeutic/translational potential:**
- Clinical applications
- Drug development implications
- Diagnostic possibilities

**Appropriate hedging:**
- ✅ "may provide a therapeutic strategy"
- ✅ "suggest potential for..."
- ✅ "could be leveraged to..."
- ❌ NOT "will cure..." (overclaiming)

**Example:**
> "Understanding the NUPR1–lipocalin-2–iron axis may facilitate development of targeted interventions that modulate iron availability to suppress tumorigenesis in high-risk populations."

**3. Conceptual advances:**
- New biological principles
- Unexpected connections
- Cross-field implications

---

### Paragraph 6 (Final): Conclusions and Future Directions

**Purpose:** Synthesis statement and specific next steps

**What to include:**

**1. Synthesis (1-2 sentences):**
- Overarching conclusion
- Lasting impact

**Example:**
> "Together, our findings demonstrate that aging-driven iron insufficiency cell-intrinsically suppresses cancer by limiting stem cell proliferative capacity, revealing an unexpected tumor-suppressive mechanism of aging."

**2. Future directions (MUST be specific):**
- Concrete experiments to perform
- Specific questions to address
- Testable hypotheses

**❌ VAGUE (avoid):**
- "More research is needed"
- "Future studies should explore this further"
- "Additional work will clarify..."

**✅ SPECIFIC (required):**
- "Future experiments should test whether NUPR1 inhibition enhances tumor susceptibility in aged mice"
- "Whether similar iron insufficiency mechanisms operate in human lung aging requires examination of clinical samples"
- "The interplay between cell-intrinsic iron limitation and systemic iron metabolism during aging warrants investigation"

---

## Discussion Editing Checklist

**Structure:**
- [ ] Paragraph 1: Summary of key findings (no new data)
- [ ] Paragraphs 2-3: Mechanistic interpretation + literature positioning
- [ ] Paragraph 4: Limitations acknowledged honestly
- [ ] Paragraph 5: Broader implications stated
- [ ] Paragraph 6: Synthesis + specific future directions

**Literature Integration:**
- [ ] Findings positioned vs. prior work (with citations)
- [ ] Similarities noted
- [ ] Differences explained
- [ ] Contradictions addressed professionally

**Interpretation Quality:**
- [ ] Mechanistic explanations provided (HOW/WHY)
- [ ] Biological logic clear
- [ ] Connections to pathways/processes explained
- [ ] NOT just repetition of Results

**Limitations:**
- [ ] Honestly stated (not hidden)
- [ ] Model system constraints acknowledged
- [ ] Generalizability discussed
- [ ] Alternative interpretations considered

**Future Directions:**
- [ ] Specific experiments proposed
- [ ] Testable questions identified
- [ ] NOT vague "more research needed"

**Voice:**
- [ ] Appropriate hedging for interpretations ("may," "suggest")
- [ ] Declarative for well-supported conclusions
- [ ] Professional tone (not defensive)
- [ ] No new data introduced

## Common Discussion Issues and Fixes

**Issue 1: Results Repetition**

**❌ WRONG (just repeating Results):**
> "We found that aged mice had 68% fewer tumors (P = 0.004). Tumors were 40% smaller (P = 0.012). Protein levels increased 2.8-fold (P = 0.003)."

**✅ CORRECT (interpretation):**
> "Our findings reveal that aging suppresses tumorigenesis through a cell-intrinsic mechanism involving iron insufficiency. This is consistent with the known requirement for iron in DNA synthesis and cell proliferation^15^, suggesting that aging-driven iron limitation directly constrains the rapid division necessary for tumor growth."

---

**Issue 2: Missing Literature Positioning**

**❌ WRONG (no citations, no context):**
> "Our results show that aging affects cancer. This is interesting."

**✅ CORRECT (positioned with citations):**
> "Our findings contrast with epidemiological data showing increased cancer incidence with age^12,13^, but align with recent reports of age-related decline in stem cell function^14,15^. This suggests that aging's effects on cancer may be context-dependent, varying between cell-intrinsic changes (suppressive) and systemic alterations (permissive)^16^."

---

**Issue 3: No Limitations**

**❌ WRONG (limitations ignored):**
> "Our study definitively proves the role of iron in aging and cancer."

**✅ CORRECT (honest limitations):**
> "While our Kras-driven lung cancer model provides clear evidence for iron's role in aging-mediated tumor suppression, whether this mechanism operates in other oncogenic contexts (e.g., EGFR-mutant, TP53-null) or tissue types requires further investigation. Additionally, our study examined mice aged 18-24 months; the effects of more advanced aging remain to be determined."

---

**Issue 4: Vague Future Directions**

**❌ WRONG (generic):**
> "Future studies should investigate this further and do more research to better understand the mechanisms."

**✅ CORRECT (specific):**
> "Future work should: (1) test whether NUPR1 inhibition enhances tumorigenesis in aged mice; (2) examine whether similar iron insufficiency occurs in human lung aging using clinical samples; and (3) determine the interplay between cell-intrinsic iron limitation and systemic iron metabolism."

---

**Issue 5: Overclaiming**

**❌ WRONG (too strong for Discussion):**
> "Our study proves that manipulating iron will cure cancer in elderly patients."

**✅ CORRECT (appropriately hedged):**
> "These findings suggest that modulating iron availability may offer a therapeutic strategy to suppress tumorigenesis in specific contexts. However, clinical translation will require careful consideration of dosing, timing, and patient selection to balance anti-tumor effects with the need for adequate iron for normal cellular function."

---

**Issue 6: Defensive Tone**

**❌ WRONG (defensive about discrepancies):**
> "Although Smith et al. found the opposite result, their study was flawed and used the wrong model, so our findings are clearly correct."

**✅ CORRECT (professional positioning):**
> "Our findings differ from those of Smith et al.^14^, who reported increased tumorigenesis with age. This discrepancy may reflect differences in experimental systems (physiologically aged mice vs. progeroid models) or oncogenic drivers (Kras vs. Myc), suggesting that aging's effects are context-dependent."

---

## Hedging Language for Discussion

**Interpretation (use hedging):**
- "may indicate"
- "suggest that"
- "consistent with"
- "is likely due to"
- "could explain"
- "points to"

**Well-supported conclusions (stronger):**
- "demonstrate"
- "reveal"
- "show"
- "establish"
- "indicate"

**Speculation (strong hedging):**
- "it is possible that"
- "one possibility is"
- "may reflect"
- "could potentially"

**Contrast/discrepancies:**
- "contrasts with"
- "differs from"
- "this discrepancy may reflect"
- "one explanation for this difference is"

## Discussion Quality Standards (CNS-Level)

**Gate 1: Interpretation Depth**
- [ ] HOW and WHY explained, not just WHAT
- [ ] Biological logic provided
- [ ] Mechanistic connections clear
- [ ] NOT just Results repetition

**Gate 2: Literature Integration**
- [ ] Positioned vs. prior work with citations
- [ ] Similarities acknowledged
- [ ] Differences explained
- [ ] Contradictions addressed

**Gate 3: Limitations Honesty**
- [ ] Model system constraints stated
- [ ] Technical limitations acknowledged
- [ ] Generalizability discussed
- [ ] Alternative interpretations considered

**Gate 4: Appropriate Hedging**
- [ ] Interpretations use "may," "suggest"
- [ ] Well-supported findings more declarative
- [ ] NOT overclaiming
- [ ] NOT overly apologetic

**Gate 5: Specific Future Directions**
- [ ] Concrete experiments proposed
- [ ] Testable questions identified
- [ ] NOT "more research needed"

## Example Discussion Paragraph

**BEFORE (Weak):**
> "Our results show aging affects cancer. Other people found similar things. More research is needed."

**AFTER (CNS-Quality):**
> "Our findings demonstrate that aging cell-intrinsically suppresses lung cancer through a NUPR1–lipocalin-2–iron insufficiency axis. This mechanism is consistent with the well-established requirement for iron in DNA synthesis enzymes^15^ and aligns with recent reports of age-related metabolic rewiring^16,17^. However, our results contrast with epidemiological data showing increased cancer incidence with age^18,19^, suggesting that aging's effects are context-dependent. While cell-intrinsic aging appears tumor-suppressive (as we show), systemic changes such as immune decline, chronic inflammation, and stromal alterations may be permissive for tumorigenesis^20^. Future work should distinguish between these competing influences by examining tumor development in aged mice with and without systemic rejuvenation interventions (e.g., heterochronic parabiosis, young blood transfusion). Additionally, whether similar iron insufficiency mechanisms operate in human lung aging requires analysis of clinical samples stratified by age and tumor status."

**Improvements:**
- ✅ Mechanistic interpretation (iron → DNA synthesis)
- ✅ Literature positioning with citations
- ✅ Contradictions addressed with explanation
- ✅ Honest acknowledgment of complexity
- ✅ Specific future directions (not "more research")
