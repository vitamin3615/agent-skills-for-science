# Long-Manuscript Protocol

Maintain coherence across a long document without imposing a fixed phase structure or creating files by default.

## Establish a compact map

Record in working memory:

- article type, target audience, and requested editing scope;
- central claim and the evidence supporting it;
- section purposes and key transitions;
- terminology, abbreviations, group names, units, and tense conventions;
- unresolved factual questions and claim-calibration risks.

For a document too large to hold reliably in context, process logical sections and retain a concise running map. Create a project-local tracking artifact only when the user requests or approves one; report its path.

## Edit by dependency

Read enough of the whole manuscript to understand the argument before making structural changes. Edit the evidence-bearing sections before finalizing the abstract, title, or cover letter so summaries reflect the revised paper. Recheck moved passages at both their old and new locations.

## Cross-section reconciliation

Verify that:

- title, abstract, Results, and Discussion state compatible claims;
- aims, methods, results, figures, and conclusions refer to the same comparisons;
- every major abstract claim is supported in the body;
- terminology, sample counts, units, and figure citations are consistent;
- limitations and null or adverse findings have not disappeared during compression;
- citations still support the propositions beside them.

## Delivery

Return the requested revision first. Summarize material structural changes and unresolved content questions. Do not create a mandatory scorecard or intermediate note file.
