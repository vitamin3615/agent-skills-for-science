# Word-Level Precision

Use the smallest change that makes the intended scientific meaning clearer.

## Preserve evidence

- Never supply an absent number, unit, group label, sample size, test, P value, confidence interval, method detail, citation, or mechanism.
- Preserve negation, direction, magnitude, uncertainty, population, comparator, and temporal scope.
- Request missing information in an editorial note rather than completing a plausible pattern.

Example:

Source: `Protein abundance was significantly higher in older animals.`

Safe edit: `Protein abundance was higher in older animals.`

Editorial note: `[If available, report the effect estimate, uncertainty, experimental unit and sample size, and analysis.]`

Do not add a fold change or P value unless it appears elsewhere in supplied material and the mapping is unambiguous.

## Choose verbs by evidence

- Use association language for observational relationships.
- Use causal verbs only when the design and analysis identify the causal claim.
- Distinguish direct measurement from interpretation (`was associated with`, `is consistent with`, `suggests`, `supports`).
- Avoid novelty superlatives such as `first`, `unprecedented`, or `paradigm-shifting` unless verified and appropriate.

## Improve clarity

- Prefer a specific entity over an ambiguous pronoun.
- Replace vague intensifiers with supplied quantitative information when it materially affects interpretation.
- Remove redundancy and nominalization when technical meaning is unchanged.
- Preserve field-standard terminology and define abbreviations at the required scope.
- Do not force active voice, a synonym, or a journal-like cadence when the original is clearer.

## Final comparison

Compare source and revision sentence by sentence. Flag any change to a scientific claim, any unresolved ambiguity, and any moved or removed content that could alter interpretation.
