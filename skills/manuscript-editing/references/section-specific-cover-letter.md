# Cover Letter Guide (Submit & Revised)

## Overview

A cover letter is a brief business letter introducing your manuscript to the journal editor. Most peer-reviewed journals require one. A well-crafted cover letter helps editors understand your work's significance and suitability for their journal.

**Two Types:**
1. **Submit Letter (First Cover Letter):** Introduces new manuscript
2. **Revised Letter (Second Cover Letter):** Accompanies revision after peer review

---

## Submit Letter Structure

### Header (Opening Salutation)
- Editor's name and position (in bold)
- Date of submission (MM-DD-YYYY)
- Journal name

### Body: Essential Content Areas

> [!NOTE]
> Structure is flexible—what matters is covering all essential content in 1 to 1.5 pages. Most letters use 2-4 paragraphs, but organize based on your needs.

**1. Manuscript Introduction** (typically one paragraph)
- Title (in bold)
- Manuscript type (original research, review, case report, opinion)
- Length, number of tables/figures (optional)
- If invited or for special issue, state this

**2. Novelty & Significance Statement** (the critical element)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is what editors care about most. Answer two questions:
> 1. **Why is this work important?** (new method, new mechanism, new finding)
> 2. **How does it advance current knowledge?** (what gap does it fill?)

**Goals to achieve:**
- Provide brief context (1-2 sentences on current state of knowledge)
- State the question/problem your study addresses
- Present your key findings and their significance
- Deliver a clear take-home message
- Explain why journal's readers would be interested (reference Aims & Scope)

**DON'T copy your abstract.** Explain significance in your own words.

**3. Required Declarations** (can be one paragraph or integrated)
- Author agreement: "All authors have read and approved the final manuscript"
- Originality: "This work has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration elsewhere"
- Conflict of interest statement
- Funding source declaration
- Ethical approvals (if applicable)
- Permission note (if reusing previously published material)

**4. Optional Elements** (include as needed)
- Suggested reviewers (with expertise areas)
- Request to exclude specific reviewers (max 3, with justification)
- Reference to prior contact with journal/editor
- List of supplementary materials

### Footer (Closing Salutation)
- "Sincerely," or "Best regards,"
- Corresponding author signature
- Contact information (affiliation, email)

## Revised Letter Structure

### Header
Same as submit letter, but include **Manuscript ID** assigned by journal.

### Body: Essential Content

**1. Gratitude & Acknowledgment**
> "We would like to thank you for the opportunity to revise and resubmit our manuscript [ID/Title]."

**Goals:**
- Express appreciation for the review process
- Acknowledge the value of reviewer feedback

**2. Response Summary**
- Provide a roadmap of major revisions made
- Reference the attached point-by-point response document
- Optionally highlight 2-3 key changes

**3. Point-by-Point Response (Attached Document)**
- Address every issue raised by editor and reviewers
- Quote each comment, then provide your response
- If you disagree, provide scientific justification (professionally)

### Footer
> "We hope the revised manuscript is now acceptable for publication in [Journal]. We look forward to hearing from you."

## Manuscript Type Adaptations

| Type | Cover Letter Emphasis |
|------|----------------------|
| **Original Research** | Specific experimental advance relative to prior work |
| **Review Article** | Synthesis and conceptual advance; what's trending (1-5 years) |
| **Opinion/Trends** | Strong, novel stance on hypothesis; future perspective |

## What Editors Actually Look For

From Nature Biomedical Engineering and Cell Press editors:

### What Helps
1. **Context → Motivation → Advance → Implications** (in that order)
2. **Type of advance:** Is it new technology? New mechanism? Improved workflow? Translational potential?
3. **Realistic scope:** What are the limitations and remaining challenges?
4. **Appropriate audience targeting:** Write for the editor, not the general reader

### What Gets Skipped
Editors quickly skip: "novel," "promising," "transformative," "major advance," "platform technology with untapped potential"

### What Hurts
- Copying the abstract verbatim
- Empty excitement without substance
- Overstated claims not supported by data
- Wrong journal or editor name (suggests rejected elsewhere)
- Long lists of excluded reviewers

## 8-Point Quality Checklist

Before submitting, verify:

| # | Check | ✓ |
|---|-------|---|
| 1 | Editor correctly named and spelled? | ☐ |
| 2 | Journal name correct (no prior journal left in)? | ☐ |
| 3 | Novelty stated substantively (not empty words)? | ☐ |
| 4 | Abstract NOT copied into letter? | ☐ |
| 5 | Length 1 to 1.5 pages (~200-400 words)? | ☐ |
| 6 | All required declarations included? | ☐ |
| 7 | Tone humble and professional? | ☐ |
| 8 | Proofread for typos and grammar? | ☐ |

## Dos and Don'ts

### ✅ DO
- Check journal's "Instructions for Authors" for specific requirements
- Use institutional letterhead if possible
- Spell editor's name correctly
- Reference journal's Aims & Scope when explaining fit
- Include appropriate citations if referencing published work
- Keep it concise (1 to 1.5 pages)
- Show genuine (not exaggerated) enthusiasm

### ❌ DON'T
- Copy/paste your abstract
- Use jargon or unexpanded acronyms
- Exaggerate or overstate findings
- Suggest friends/colleagues as reviewers
- Exclude reviewers from "entire continents"
- Include personal accolades or past accomplishments
- Be rude or overly complimentary to editors
- Write a generic letter usable for any journal

## Template: Submit Letter

```
[Date]

[Editor's Name], [Position]
[Journal Name]

Dear [Editor's Name],

We are pleased to submit our [manuscript type], entitled "[Title]," for 
consideration in [Journal Name]. This manuscript includes [X] words, 
[Y] figures, and [Z] tables.

[1-2 sentences: Current state of knowledge and the gap your study addresses.]
[2-3 sentences: What you did and what you found.]
[1-2 sentences: Why this matters—the take-home message.]
We believe this work will interest [Journal Name] readers because 
[specific reference to journal's scope/recent publications].

All authors have read and approved the final manuscript. This work has 
not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another 
journal. [The authors declare no competing interests. / Competing 
interests are disclosed in the manuscript.] This work was supported by 
[funding source]. [Ethical approval was obtained from X.]

[Optional: We suggest the following reviewers with relevant expertise: 
1) Name, Institution, Expertise; 2) Name, Institution, Expertise.]

Sincerely,

[Signature]
[Corresponding Author Name], [Title]
[Affiliation]
[Email]
```

## CNS-Specific Considerations

For Cell/Nature/Science-tier journals:

1. **Be specific about the advance:** Editors handle many submissions; generic "novel" claims are ineffective
2. **Demonstrate journal fit:** Show you know the journal's recent publications
3. **Address breadth:** CNS journals want broad interest—explain interdisciplinary implications
4. **Anticipate assessment criteria:** Help editor identify degree of advance, implications, rigor
5. **State limitations honestly:** This builds credibility and aids evaluation
