# The 8-Question Framework for CNS-Quality Editing

**Core philosophy:** CNS-quality writing demonstrates sophisticated scientific thinking. This framework teaches HOW CNS editors think, not just template compliance.

**When evaluating ANY paragraph, ask these 8 questions:**

---

## 1. Is the Hierarchy Clear?

**Question:** Are the most important findings emphasized first?

**Good hierarchy:**

> "dITP activates KomBC most robustly (Fig. 3A). Although IDP also activated KomBC, it triggered a >100-fold-weaker response compared with dITP, and other tested nucleotides did not activate KomBC."

**Why this works:** Strongest finding first → partial finding → quantitative comparison → negatives stated.

---

## 2. Is Quantification Precise?

**Question:** Are fold-changes, percentages, or absolute values provided?

**Precision spectrum:**

- ❌ Bad: "increased"
- ⚠️ Better: "increased significantly (P < 0.05)"
- ✅ Best: "increased 4.2-fold (P = 0.003, n = 6)" OR "strongly correlated (Fig. 2a)"

**Avoid vague quantifiers:** "significantly," "greatly," "many," "several" → Replace with specific numbers OR reference the supporting Figure/Table.

**See:** [word-level-precision.md](word-level-precision.md)

---

## 3. Are Statistics Complete?

**For every quantitative claim, verify:**

- ☐ n value with units (n = 6 biological replicates) OR reference to Figure/Table
- ☐ P value (exact when P > 0.001) OR reference to Figure/Table
- ☐ Statistical test named OR reference to Figure/Table
- ☐ Error bar type (s.d. vs. s.e.m.)

**Note:** While inline statistics are preferred for key findings, referencing a Figure/Table that contains full statistical details is acceptable.

---

## 4. Is Mechanism Integrated?

**Question:** Is the "how/why" woven into narrative, or awkwardly bolted on?

**Poor integration:** "Treatment increased expression. The mechanism involves NUPR1. NUPR1 regulates lipocalin-2."

**Excellent integration:** "Treatment increased expression by activating NUPR1, which induces lipocalin-2 and leads to functional iron insufficiency."

---

## 5. Are Negatives Acknowledged?

**Question:** Are null/unexpected results reported professionally?

**Professional reporting:**

> "However, RP did not significantly increase mature neuron survival and, instead, led to a twofold increase in astrocyte survival."

**Why this works:** Acknowledges negative, no defensiveness, pivots to unexpected positive.

---

## 6. Does It Flow Naturally?

**Question:** Does it read like a narrative story, or like a checklist?

**Tools for flow:**

- Contrast structures: "However, unlike..."
- Progressive structures: "We first show...We then demonstrate..."
- Causal connectors: "leading to," "resulting in"
- Hierarchical phrases: "most robustly," "Although"

---

## 7. Is Claim Strength Calibrated?

**Critical skill:** Match verb strength to evidence strength.

**Evidence → Claim Matrix:**

| Evidence Type            | Appropriate Verbs                   | NEVER Use            |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Correlation only**     | correlates with, is associated with | regulates, causes    |
| **Loss-of-function**     | is required for, is necessary for   | is sufficient for    |
| **Gain-of-function**     | is sufficient for, can induce       | is required for      |
| **Loss + gain + rescue** | demonstrates, proves, establishes   | suggests (too weak)  |
| **Preliminary/indirect** | consistent with, may suggest        | demonstrates, proves |

**See complete calibration guide:** [word-level-precision.md](word-level-precision.md). For conventional phrasing examples, see [academic-phrases.md](academic-phrases.md) (Section 2).

---

## 8. Is the Prose Clear and Effective?

**Question:** Is each sentence as clear and concise as possible, with appropriate vocabulary?

**Evaluate:**

- **Sentence structure:** Are sentences easy to parse on first reading?
- **Word choice:** Are words precise and effective (not just "technically accurate")?
- **Vocabulary register:** Does word choice match CNS paper conventions?
- **Conciseness:** Can the same meaning be expressed more economically?
- **Scientific English:** Does it follow standard conventions?

**Prose quality spectrum:**

| Level         | Characteristic                   | Action       |
| ------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Clear**     | Meaning evident on first read    | Preserve     |
| **Adequate**  | Understandable but could improve | Enhance      |
| **Unclear**   | Requires re-reading or inference | Rewrite      |
| **Confusing** | Meaning ambiguous or obscured    | Must rewrite |

**Key principle:** "Technically accurate" ≠ "good writing." Unclear prose that is factually correct still needs revision. Check [academic-phrases.md](academic-phrases.md) for conventional discourse patterns.

## Quick Application Checklist

When evaluating any paragraph, mentally check:

1. ☐ Hierarchy clear? (Important findings first)
2. ☐ Quantification precise? (Numbers, not vague words)
3. ☐ Statistics complete? (n, P, test, error bars)
4. ☐ Mechanism integrated? (How/why woven in)
5. ☐ Negatives acknowledged? (Professional reporting)
6. ☐ Flow natural? (Narrative, not checklist)
7. ☐ Claims calibrated? (Verbs match evidence)
8. ☐ Prose clear? (Meaning evident on first read)

---

## Example Application

```
❌ UNCLEAR (but technically accurate):
   "The investigation of the cellular response to the treatment
    revealed outcomes that were consistent with expectations."

✅ CLEAR:
   "Treatment induced the expected cellular response."
```

**Why the fix works:** Shorter, active voice, direct statement. Same meaning, half the words.
