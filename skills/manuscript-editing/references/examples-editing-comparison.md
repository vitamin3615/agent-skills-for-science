# Editing Comparisons

These examples illustrate meaning-preserving revision. A real edit should use only information supplied in the manuscript or explicitly provided by the author.

## Abstract

Source:
> We studied whether aging changes tumor development in mice. Older mice developed fewer and smaller tumors. The results suggest that altered iron availability may contribute.

Edited:
> We tested whether aging alters tumor development in mice. Older mice developed fewer and smaller tumors, and the results implicate altered iron availability as a possible contributor.

Why: the revision improves flow and removes repetition without inventing effect sizes, molecular actors, causal mechanisms, sample sizes, or statistics. If those details are needed, request them separately.

## Results

Source:
> Immunoblotting showed higher SUV39H1 abundance in samples from older mice. The difference was statistically significant.

Edited:
> SUV39H1 abundance was higher in samples from older mice, as measured by immunoblotting. `[Please report the effect estimate, uncertainty, experimental unit and sample size, test, and exact P value if available.]`

Why: the finding is stated directly, while missing quantitative information remains an editorial query rather than invented prose.

## Discussion

Source:
> Aging was associated with fewer tumors in this model. Iron availability might be involved, but more research is needed.

Edited:
> In this model, aging was associated with fewer tumors. The results raise the possibility that iron availability contributes to this association, but they do not establish causality or generalizability beyond the model studied.

Why: the revision names the evidential boundary and preserves uncertainty. It does not turn an association into a mechanism or introduce an unsupported clinical implication.

## Methods

Source:
> We measured protein abundance by immunoblotting using commercial antibodies and standard procedures.

Edited:
> We measured protein abundance by immunoblotting using commercial antibodies. `[Please provide antibody identifiers, incubation and detection conditions, normalization procedures, and analysis details needed for reproducibility.]`

Why: vague wording is removed, but missing procedures are requested rather than guessed.

## What not to infer

Do not infer that an omitted value exists, that a conventional method was used, or that a statistically significant statement implies a particular threshold or test. Do not add figure numbers, replicate types, database identifiers, software versions, causal verbs, or novelty claims unless the source supports them.
