## Quick Reference by Category

### 1. Sequential/Temporal
**Beginning:** First, Initially, To begin with, At the outset
**Progress:** Subsequently, Next, Following this, Then, Afterwards
**Final:** Finally, Ultimately, Lastly, In the end

**CNS patterns:**
- "We first examined..."
- "Following this observation..."
- "Subsequently, we tested..."
- "Finally, we assessed..."

### 2. Causal (Cause → Effect)
**Cause:** Because, Since, Due to, Given that, Owing to
**Effect:** Therefore, Thus, Hence, Consequently, As a result
**Purpose:** To [verb], In order to, With the aim of

**CNS patterns:**
- "...thereby [outcome]"
- "...which in turn [result]"
- "...leading to [consequence]"
- "...resulting in [effect]"
- "As a result of [X], we observed [Y]"

### 3. Contrast/Opposition
**Strong:** However, Nevertheless, Conversely, In contrast
**Moderate:** Although, While, Despite, Yet, Still
**Comparison:** By contrast, On the other hand, Whereas

**CNS patterns:**
- "However, [X] remains unclear..."
- "Despite [progress], [gap] persists"
- "In contrast to [prior work], we found..."
- "Whereas [X showed Y], [Z demonstrated W]"

### 4. Addition/Extension
**Simple:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition
**Emphasis:** Importantly, Notably, Significantly, Remarkably
**Support:** Indeed, In fact, Consistent with this

**CNS patterns:**
- "Moreover, [additional finding]"
- "Importantly, [key observation]"
- "Consistent with this notion, [supporting data]"
- "In addition to [X], we observed [Y]"

### 5. Exemplification
**Introducing:** For example, For instance, Specifically
**Highlighting:** In particular, Particularly, Especially, Notably

**CNS patterns:**
- "For example, [specific case]"
- "Specifically, we found that..."
- "In particular, [protein/pathway] showed..."

### 6. Emphasis/Highlighting
**Strong:** Critically, Crucially, Importantly, Notably
**Moderate:** Significantly, Remarkably, Interestingly
**Unexpected:** Surprisingly, Unexpectedly, Strikingly

**CNS patterns:**
- "Importantly, [key finding]..."
- "Notably, [observation]..."
- "Remarkably, [unexpected result]..."

### 7. Similarity/Agreement
**Strong:** Similarly, Likewise, Equally, Correspondingly
**Support:** Consistent with, In agreement with, In line with
**Confirmation:** Indeed, As expected, Confirming this

**CNS patterns:**
- "Consistent with previous work..."
- "Similarly, [X] also showed..."
- "In agreement with [reference], we observed..."

### 8. Summary/Conclusion
**Synthesizing:** Together, Collectively, In summary, Overall
**Concluding:** Thus, Therefore, Hence, Consequently
**Inferring:** These results suggest/indicate/demonstrate

**CNS patterns:**
- "Together, these findings reveal..."
- "Collectively, our data indicate..."
- "These results demonstrate that..."
- "Thus, [conclusion]..."

### 9. Limitation/Qualification
**Hedging:** May, Might, Could, Likely, Potentially, Possibly
**Constraining:** Although, However, Despite, Notwithstanding
**Uncertainty:** It remains unclear whether, Whether [X] requires further investigation

**CNS patterns:**
- "Although [caveat], our data suggest..."
- "While these findings indicate [X], whether [Y] remains unclear..."
- "These data may suggest..."

### 10. Interpretation/Inference
**Suggesting:** Suggest(s) that, Indicate(s) that, Imply/Implies
**Demonstrating:** Demonstrate(s), Show(s), Reveal(s), Establish(es)
**Explaining:** This explains, This accounts for, This is consistent with

**CNS patterns:**
- "These findings suggest that..."
- "Our data demonstrate that..."
- "This result is consistent with a model whereby..."

## Section-Specific Transition Patterns

### Abstract Transitions
- Context → Gap: "However, **whether/how** [X] remains **unclear/unknown**"
- Gap → Study: "**Here we** [approach] to [goal]"
- Finding → Finding: "Additionally, Moreover, Furthermore"
- Finding → Impact: "These findings, Our results"

### Introduction Transitions
- Paragraph 1→2: "To [function], [process] requires..."
- Known → Gap: "**However**, **Despite**, **Although**"
- Gap → Study: "**Here we**, **To address this**, **We sought to**"

### Results Transitions
- Finding → Finding: "In addition to, Moreover, Furthermore, Next, Subsequently"
- Finding → Validation: "To test this, To validate, Confirming this"
- Finding → Mechanism: "Mechanistically, This occurs through, This is mediated by"
- Paragraph → Paragraph: Declarative subheading + topic sentence

### Discussion Transitions
- Summary → Interpretation: "These findings suggest, Our results indicate"
- Finding → Literature: "Consistent with, In contrast to, Extending"
- Strength → Limitation: "However, Although, Despite, While"
- Current → Future: "Future studies, Further investigation, It remains to be determined"

## Transition Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Overuse
**Problem:** Repetitive transitions
**Example:** "Furthermore, X. Furthermore, Y. Furthermore, Z."
**Fix:** Vary transitions: "Moreover, X. Additionally, Y. Consistent with this, Z."

### ❌ Weak Connections
**Problem:** Generic transitions that don't show logical relationship
**Example:** "We did X. Also, we did Y."
**Fix:** "We first examined X. To validate this finding, we next tested Y."

### ❌ Contradiction Without Acknowledgment
**Problem:** Contradictory statements without "However"
**Example:** "X increased. Y decreased."
**Fix:** "X increased. By contrast, Y decreased."

### ❌ Missing Transitions
**Problem:** Abrupt topic shifts
**Example:** "Protein A regulates X. Protein B is important."
**Fix:** "Protein A regulates X. In addition to A, Protein B also contributes..."

## Transition Selection Guide

**To show progression:** First → Subsequently → Finally
**To show cause:** Because, Since → Therefore, Thus, As a result
**To contrast:** However, Nevertheless, Conversely, By contrast
**To add:** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, In addition
**To exemplify:** For example, Specifically, In particular
**To emphasize:** Importantly, Notably, Critically, Remarkably
**To agree:** Similarly, Likewise, Consistent with, In agreement
**To summarize:** Together, Collectively, In summary, Overall
**To hedge:** May, Might, Could, Potentially, Likely
**To interpret:** Suggest, Indicate, Demonstrate, Reveal
