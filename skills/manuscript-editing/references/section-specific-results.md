# Results-Specific Editing Guidelines

**Purpose:** Present experimental findings with complete statistical rigor, figure integration, and quantitative precision.

**Key principle:** Results = Observations ONLY. Save interpretations for Discussion.

---

## Critical Requirements for Results

### 1. Declarative Subheadings (The "Headline" Principle)

**Purpose:** Summarize the section's main conclusion as a standalone sentence.

**Standard:** Use active voice, specific actors, and directional verbs. Avoid passive constructions or simple topic labels.

**❌ BAD (Topic-based or Passive):**

- "Analysis of aged AT2 cells" (Topic)
- "Tumorigenesis is reduced in aged mice" (Passive)
- "Effect of NUPR1 on stemness" (Vague)

**✅ GOOD (Declarative Conclusion):**

- "Ageing limits stemness and tumorigenesis by reprogramming iron homeostasis" (Active, mechanistic)
- "NUPR1 inactivation rescues stemness in aged cells" (Specific intervention → Outcome)
- "Aspartate signalling drives lung metastasis via alternative translation" (Mechanism → Phenotype)

**Patterns from High-Impact Papers:**

- **Action → Result:** "Iron chelation blunted the increase in tumour sphere formation"
- **Mechanism → Consequence:** "DNA hypomethylation underpins ageing-associated induction of the NUPR1 axis"
- **Contrast:** "Young mice showed higher grade adenocarcinomas than aged mice"

**Rule:** State the *biological conclusion*, not the experimental procedure.

---

### 2. Statistical Rigor (MANDATORY—Most Critical Aspect)

**For EVERY quantitative claim, you MUST include EITHER inline statistics OR a reference to a Figure/Table containing them:**

**☐ n value (sample size) with units:**

- "n = 6 mice"
- "n = 8 biological replicates"
- "n = 3 independent experiments"
- "n = 120 cells analyzed"

**☐ P value:**

- Exact when P > 0.001: "P = 0.032"
- Range when P < 0.001: "P < 0.001" or "P = 0.0003"
- NOT just "P < 0.05" (too vague)

**☐ Statistical test explicitly named:**

- "Student's t-test"
- "two-tailed Student's t-test"
- "one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc test"
- "Mann–Whitney U test" (for non-parametric)
- "log-rank test" (for survival)
- "chi-square test" (for categorical)
- "two-way ANOVA"

**☐ Error bars defined:**

- "mean ± s.d." (standard deviation)
- "mean ± s.e.m." (standard error of the mean)
- "median with interquartile range"
- "mean with 95% confidence interval"

**Complete statistical notation example:**

> "Aged mice exhibited a 68% reduction in tumor number (Fig. 1c; n = 8 mice per group; P = 0.004, two-tailed Student's t-test; data show mean ± s.d.)"

**Incomplete (DO NOT accept):**

- ❌ "significantly increased" (no numbers, no test, no n)
- ❌ "P < 0.05" (too vague, no test named)
- ❌ "n = 6, P = 0.03" (test not named)
- ❌ "increased 2-fold (P < 0.001)" (n missing, test missing)

---

### 3. Figure Integration (MANDATORY)

**Every data claim must reference a figure or table:**

**Format:** "(Fig. 2a)" or "(Extended Data Fig. 5c)"

- Panel letters REQUIRED: "2a" not just "Figure 2"
- Specific panels: "Fig. 3a,b" for multiple panels
- Supplementary: "Extended Data Fig. 4c"

**Placement:** Immediately after the claim

**Example:**

> "SUV39H1 protein levels were increased by 2.8-fold in aged cells (Fig. 3a; n = 8 biological replicates; P = 0.003, Student's t-test)."

**Why critical:**

- Allows readers to evaluate data directly
- Connects narrative to evidence
- Essential for peer review

---

### 4. Quantitative Precision

**Core principle:** Quantification requirements depend on the type of claim and context.

---

#### 4.1 MANDATORY Quantification (with complete statistics)

**For direct experimental comparisons**, ALWAYS provide specific numbers WITH complete statistics:

**❌ UNACCEPTABLE:**

- "significantly increased" (no fold-change, no statistics)
- "greatly reduced" (no percentage, no statistics)
- "higher levels" (no quantification, no statistics)

**✅ REQUIRED:**

- "increased 2.3-fold (n = 6; P = 0.03, Student's t-test)"
- "reduced by 47% (n = 8; P = 0.008, Mann–Whitney test)"
- "higher levels by 3.2-fold (n = 10; P < 0.001, ANOVA)"

**This applies to:**

- Primary experimental findings reported with statistical tests
- Direct comparisons between experimental groups
- Measurements of specific experimental outcomes

---

#### 4.2 PREFERRED Quantification (context-dependent)

**For specific experimental measurements**, provide quantification when available, but recognize that qualitative comparative terms are acceptable in these contexts:

**✅ ACCEPTABLE in CNS papers:**

- "most robustly" / "most abundant" / "highest" (when describing relative patterns)
- "predominantly involved in" (when describing categorical distributions)
- "markedly extended" / "substantially increased" (when accompanied by statistics elsewhere)
- "fastest growth rate" / "strongest preservation" (when overall pattern is clear)
- "in most models" / "most samples" (when exact counts aren't the focus)

**When to add quantification:**

- If specific values are readily available and add clarity
- If the magnitude is important for the scientific claim
- If readers need the exact value to evaluate the finding

**When qualitative terms are acceptable:**

- Describing relative comparisons or rankings
- Summarizing patterns across complex datasets
- Improving readability while maintaining scientific accuracy
- When the specific number doesn't change the interpretation

---

#### 4.3 Specific Quantification Patterns

**When you DO quantify, use these patterns:**

**Fold changes:**

- "increased 2.3-fold"
- "reduced by 4-fold"
- "enhanced 10-fold"

**Percentages:**

- "47% reduction"
- "63% ± 23% of cells"
- "78% survival rate"

**Ranges:**

- "54-73% reduction across all time points"
- "between 2.1-fold and 4.8-fold"

**Comparative:**

- ">100-fold weaker response compared with X"
- "3-fold greater than control"

**Absolute values with units:**

- "10 μM concentration"
- "500 cells per field"
- "2.5 mg kg⁻¹ dose"

---

#### 4.4 Decision Framework for Quantification

**Ask these questions:**

1. **Is this a statistical comparison?** → YES: MANDATORY quantification + complete statistics
2. **Is the specific magnitude critical to the claim?** → YES: Add quantification
3. **Is the value readily available and improves clarity?** → YES: Add quantification
4. **Is this a descriptive/relative comparison?** → Qualitative terms acceptable
5. **Does the pattern matter more than exact values?** → Qualitative terms acceptable

**Example applications:**

✅ **Good use of qualitative term:**

> "Gene ontology analysis showed that most modules were significantly preserved (6 of 7; P < 0.001, permutation test), with M1, M2, and M3 showing the strongest preservation."

_Why acceptable:_ "Most" is quantified (6 of 7), significance is documented, and "strongest" describes a relative pattern without needing exact Z-scores for each module.

✅ **Good use of quantification:**

> "SUV39H1 levels were increased by 2.8-fold in aged cells (Fig. 3a; n = 8; P = 0.003, Student's t-test)."

_Why required:_ This is a primary experimental finding with a statistical comparison.

❌ **Missing required quantification:**

> "Protein levels were significantly different between groups."

_Why unacceptable:_ Primary experimental finding lacks fold-change and complete statistics.

---

### 5. Multi-Method Validation

**For major claims, show validation with ≥2 independent methods:**

**Example:**

> "SUV39H1 levels increased with age by Western blot (2.8-fold; Fig. 3a; P = 0.003) and by immunofluorescence quantification (3.1-fold; Fig. 3b; P = 0.001)."

**Common validation approaches:**

- Genetic + pharmacological
- In vitro + in vivo
- Protein (Western) + RNA (qPCR)
- Microscopy + biochemical assay

**When validation is missing:**

- Note in editorial summary: "Consider validating finding X with independent method"
- DO NOT fabricate second method

---

### 6. Results Paragraph Structure

**Typical CNS Results paragraph flow:**

**1. Opening sentence: State main finding**

- Declarative, specific
- Include quantification
- Reference figure

**2. Supporting sentences: Provide details**

- Additional measurements
- Multi-method validation
- Consistency across conditions

**3. Closing sentence: Connect to next finding (optional)**

- Transition to mechanism
- Set up next experiment
- OR just end with observation

**Example:**

> "Aged KP mice exhibited a 68% reduction in tumor number compared with young mice at 12 weeks after tumor initiation (Fig. 1c; n = 6 aged and 8 young mice; P = 0.004, two-tailed Student's t-test). In addition to reduced initiation frequency, tumors that formed in aged mice were 40% smaller by volume (Extended Data Fig. 2e; n = 10 mice per group; P = 0.012, Mann–Whitney test). The proliferative index, measured by Ki67 staining, was significantly lower in aged tumors at all time points examined (Extended Data Fig. 2f; P < 0.001, one-way ANOVA). These findings indicate that aging cell-intrinsically suppresses both tumor initiation and progression."

**Note:** Final sentence provides minimal interpretation—appropriate for Results.

## Results Editing Checklist

**Statistical Rigor:**

- [ ] ALL quantitative claims have n = X (or reference Figure)
- [ ] ALL comparisons have P values (or reference Figure)
- [ ] ALL tests explicitly named (or reference Figure)
- [ ] Error bars defined (s.d., s.e.m., etc.)
- [ ] Exact P values when P > 0.001

**Figure Integration:**

- [ ] Every data point references a figure
- [ ] Panel letters specified (Fig. 2a, not just Fig. 2)
- [ ] Extended Data figures referenced when appropriate

**Quantification:**

- [ ] NO vague quantifiers ("significantly," "greatly")
- [ ] Fold-changes or percentages provided
- [ ] Specific numbers replace "many," "several," "most"
- [ ] Concentrations/doses have units

**Voice:**

- [ ] Declarative statements predominate
- [ ] Active voice where appropriate
- [ ] Results stated confidently (not overly hedged)

**Structure:**

- [ ] Subheadings describe findings, not methods
- [ ] Logical flow: finding → validation → mechanism
- [ ] Each paragraph has clear main point
- [ ] Transitions smooth between paragraphs

**Interpretations:**

- [ ] Minimal interpretation (observations prioritized)
- [ ] Save mechanistic discussion for Discussion section
- [ ] Avoid "These results suggest..." (Discussion language)

## Common Results Issues and Fixes

**Issue 1: Missing Statistics**

**❌ WRONG:**

> "Aged mice had fewer tumors than young mice."

**✅ CORRECT:**

> "Aged mice exhibited a 68% reduction in tumor number (Fig. 1c; n = 8 mice; P = 0.004, Student's t-test)."

---

**Issue 2: Unnamed Statistical Tests**

**❌ WRONG:**

> "Protein levels were significantly higher (n = 6; P = 0.03)."

**✅ CORRECT:**

> "Protein levels were increased by 2.3-fold (n = 6 biological replicates; P = 0.03, two-tailed Student's t-test)."

---

**Issue 3: Vague Quantification**

**❌ WRONG:**

> "Treatment greatly reduced tumor burden in most mice."

**✅ CORRECT:**

> "Treatment reduced tumor burden by 54% in 78% of mice (n = 18 mice; P = 0.008, Mann–Whitney test)."

---

**Issue 4: Missing Figure References**

**❌ WRONG:**

> "SUV39H1 levels increased in aged cells (n = 8; P = 0.003)."

**✅ CORRECT:**

> "SUV39H1 levels increased by 2.8-fold in aged cells (Fig. 3a; n = 8 biological replicates; P = 0.003, Student's t-test)."

---

**Issue 5: Too Much Interpretation**

**❌ WRONG (too much Discussion in Results):**

> "These findings suggest that aging may suppress cancer through iron-mediated mechanisms, which could have important implications for understanding cancer in elderly populations."

**✅ CORRECT (minimal interpretation):**

> "These findings indicate that aging cell-intrinsically suppresses tumor initiation and progression."

---

**Issue 6: Methods-Focused Subheadings**

**❌ WRONG:**

> "## Western blot analysis of protein levels"

**✅ CORRECT:**

> "## SUV39H1 protein levels increase with age"

## Statistical Notation Quick Reference

| Data Type                   | Appropriate Test    | Example Notation                               |
| --------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Two groups, continuous      | Student's t-test    | P = 0.032, two-tailed Student's t-test         |
| Two groups, non-normal      | Mann–Whitney U test | P = 0.018, Mann–Whitney test                   |
| Multiple groups             | One-way ANOVA       | P < 0.001, one-way ANOVA with Tukey's post hoc |
| Multiple groups, non-normal | Kruskal-Wallis      | P = 0.012, Kruskal-Wallis test                 |
| Two variables               | Two-way ANOVA       | P < 0.001, two-way ANOVA                       |
| Survival data               | Log-rank test       | P = 0.004, log-rank test                       |
| Categorical data            | Chi-square test     | P = 0.028, chi-square test                     |
| Paired data                 | Paired t-test       | P = 0.015, paired Student's t-test             |
| Correlation                 | Pearson or Spearman | r = 0.78, P = 0.003, Pearson correlation       |

**Error bar notation:**

- "Data show mean ± s.d."
- "Bars represent mean ± s.e.m."
- "Box plots show median with interquartile range"

## Hierarchy of Evidence in Results

**Present findings in order of strength:**

**1. Strongest findings first:**

> "dITP activates KomBC most robustly (Fig. 3A)."

**2. Partial findings second:**

> "Although IDP also activated KomBC, it triggered a >100-fold-weaker response compared with dITP."

**3. Negative findings third:**

> "Other tested nucleotides did not activate KomBC."

**Why hierarchy matters:**

- Emphasizes most important discoveries
- Provides context for weaker findings
- Professional reporting of negatives

## Results Quality Standards (CNS-Level)

**Gate 1: Complete Statistics**

- [ ] Every quantitative claim: n, P, test name, error bars (or referenced)
- [ ] No vague "significant" without values
- [ ] Exact P values when appropriate

**Gate 2: Figure Integration**

- [ ] Every claim tied to figure
- [ ] Panel letters specified
- [ ] Extended Data referenced

**Gate 3: Quantitative Precision**

- [ ] Fold-changes or percentages throughout
- [ ] NO "greatly," "markedly," "significantly" alone
- [ ] Specific numbers replace vague quantifiers

**Gate 4: Multi-Method Validation**

- [ ] Major claims supported by ≥2 approaches
- [ ] Genetic + pharmacological where relevant
- [ ] In vitro + in vivo confirmation

**Gate 5: Minimal Interpretation**

- [ ] Observations prioritized
- [ ] Save mechanistic discussion for Discussion
- [ ] Only brief connections between findings

## Example Results Paragraph Transformation

**BEFORE (Weak):**

> "We looked at protein levels in old and young mice. Western blots showed increased SUV39H1 in aged mice. The differences were significant. We think this is important."

**Problems:**

- No quantification
- No statistics (n, P, test)
- No figure reference
- "We think" = interpretation, not observation
- Passive "looked at" instead of declarative finding

---

**AFTER (CNS-Quality):**

> "SUV39H1 protein levels were increased by 2.8-fold in aged AT2 cells compared with young cells (Fig. 3a; n = 8 biological replicates; P = 0.003, two-tailed Student's t-test; data show mean ± s.d.). Immunofluorescence quantification confirmed this elevation across all aged samples (Extended Data Fig. 5b; P < 0.001, one-way ANOVA). H3K9me3 levels, the product of SUV39H1 enzymatic activity, showed a parallel 2.1-fold increase in aged cells (Fig. 3c; P = 0.008)."

**Improvements:**

- ✅ Specific quantification (2.8-fold, 2.1-fold)
- ✅ Complete statistics (n, P, test names, error bars)
- ✅ Figure integration (Fig. 3a, Extended Data Fig. 5b, Fig. 3c)
- ✅ Multi-method validation (Western + immunofluorescence)
- ✅ Declarative statements
- ✅ Removed interpretation ("we think")
- ✅ Connected findings logically (protein → product)
