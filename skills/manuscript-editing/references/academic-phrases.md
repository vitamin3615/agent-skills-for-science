# Academic Convention Patterns for Manuscript Editing

## Purpose
This reference demonstrates conventional vocabulary and phrasing patterns in high-impact biomedical writing (CNS-level). Use these examples to:
1. **Choose appropriate vocabulary** that matches evidence strength.
2. **Recognize conventional discourse patterns** in expert scientific writing.
3. **Improve author's phrasing** while preserving their voice and message.
4. **Calibrate register and formality** to journal standards.

**IMPORTANT**: These are EXAMPLES of conventions, not templates to fill in. The pattern should emerge from multiple complete examples showing how expert writers express similar concepts.

---

## 1. Establishing Research Context

### 1.1 Situating Within Field Knowledge

**Function**: Place current research within existing scientific understanding.

**Pattern A: Acknowledging established knowledge while noting evolution**
*   "A broader picture is emerging regarding microglial diversity in neurodegeneration, with recent studies revealing context-dependent phenotypes beyond the classical M1/M2 dichotomy."
*   "Our understanding of tau pathology has evolved substantially over the past decade, with new evidence challenging the simple aggregation hypothesis."
*   "Although it is well established that microglia prune synapses during development, these studies are important to understand whether this mechanism is reactivated in Alzheimer's disease."
*   "Considerable strides in immunotherapy during the acute phase of sepsis have brought improved overall survival rates."

**Pattern B: Framing current paradigm shifts**
*   "The discovery of TREM2 variants in Alzheimer's disease has fundamentally reshaped our understanding of innate immunity's role in neurodegeneration."
*   "Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics have opened new horizons for mapping cellular heterogeneity in diseased tissue."
*   "The understanding of glioblastoma heterogeneity was fundamentally reshaped following the identification of developmental hierarchies."

### 1.2 Identifying Knowledge Gaps

**Function**: Highlighting what is missing to justify the current study.

**Pattern: The "Unknown Mechanism" Gap**
*   "However, the precise mechanisms driving their effects remain incompletely understood, hindering clinical translation."
*   "The detailed molecular processes through which cells temporally and spatially coordinate such a diverse set of hallmarks will require further research."
*   "Despite over a century of amyloid research, we lack effective treatments or cures, and those that have been proposed remain controversial."

**Pattern: The "Discrepancy" Gap**
*   "Although several studies support the hypothesis that lipid metabolism drives inflammation, more recent studies have revealed distinct, non-inflammatory roles for lipids in tissue repair."
*   "Discrepancies among studies may also stem from differences in the timing of measurements, mouse models, and methods of modulation."
*   "It is currently unclear whether amyloid-beta accumulation directly leads to tau hyperphosphorylation or whether these are parallel processes."

### 1.3 Highlighting Emerging Understanding

**Function**: Introducing new concepts or consensus.

*   "Emerging evidence suggests that these circadian rhythms affect the behaviour of infiltrating immune cells."
*   "It is increasingly apparent that a broader web of metabolic pathways determines the timing and nature of the immune response."
*   "Recent studies have shed light on the intricate nature of neurovascular coupling and its crucial role in regulating clearance."

## 2. Calibrating Claim Strength

**Editing Principle**: This category supports the **MESSAGE preservation tier**. Claim strength must match evidence type. Using conventional vocabulary for different evidence levels helps calibrate claims appropriately without changing the author's scientific conclusions.

### 2.1 Strong Evidence ("Demonstrate", "Establish")

**Usage**: Use when you have loss-of-function + gain-of-function + rescue, or clear mechanisms.

*   "Recent studies have demonstrated the essential roles of TREM2 in maintaining microglial metabolic fitness."
*   "We provide clear evidence demonstrating that 7-dehydrocholesterol dictates ferroptosis sensitivity."
*   "These results demonstrate the importance of healthy lymphatic function in maintaining brain fluid homeostasis."
*   "Experiments using a fate-mapping model demonstrated a key role of monocytes in secondary inflammation."
*   "Collectively, these studies demonstrate that the inadequate metabolic environment greatly affects the integrity and function of T cells."

### 2.2 Moderate Evidence ("Suggest", "Indicate")

**Usage**: Use for strong correlations, single-method findings, or logical inferences.

*   "These findings suggest that compartmentalization of signaling components occurs especially when inflammation persists."
*   "Our data suggest that the loss of gene X protects cells through an unknown mechanism."
*   "These findings are consistent with a model in which microglia regulate neuronal activity."
*   "This observation suggests that lipid accumulation might partly reflect a lack of sensitivity of biomarkers to detect early pathology."
*   "These results indicate that distinct cancer ecosystems hijack two evolutionary paths."

### 2.3 Preliminary Evidence ("Consistent with", "Raise the possibility")

**Usage**: Use for indirect evidence, partial data, or interesting but unproven observations.

*   "This correlation raises the possibility that sleep disruption influences Alzheimer's disease progression."
*   "These findings support the possibility that systemic factors trigger the complaints in Long COVID."
*   "Preliminary epidemiological evidence suggests that the effects of climate change will aggravate neurological symptoms."
*   "These findings are consistent with the role of lipids in mobilizing immune components."

### 2.4 Expressing Uncertainty ("Remains unclear", "Poorly understood")

**Usage**: Acknowledge incomplete understanding while framing research questions.

*   "The precise mechanisms driving tau propagation between neurons remain poorly understood."
*   "How circadian disruption influences Alzheimer's disease progression is incompletely characterized."
*   "The relative contribution of peripheral vs. central immunity remains poorly understood and in need of further investigation."
*   "It is currently unclear whether this form of communication is able to modulate core processes."

## 3. Acknowledging Complexity & Limitations

### 3.1 Recognizing Nuance & Context-Dependence

**Function**: Moving beyond binary "all or nothing" statements.

*   "It seems that the context matters, perhaps reflecting the different metabolic demands of the tissue."
*   "In some contexts, microglia are protective, whereas in others they drive neurotoxicity."
*   "The answer is likely context-specific and cell-specific."
*   "Of note, the response of the immune system to stress is not uniform, indicating that the process has tissue-specific effects."

### 3.2 Noting Methodological Caveats

**Function**: Honest reporting of study limitations (crucial for CNS papers).

*   "Although this study benefited from a large cohort, it also included retrospective data, limiting its generalizability."
*   "Clinical trials are conducted under controlled conditions, limiting their generalizability to real-world populations."
*   "Comparison of incidence across trials is substantially complicated by additional differences in drug dosage."
*   "The cross-sectional nature of post-mortem human studies precludes drawing firm conclusions regarding the cause and consequences of the disease."
*   "However, the extent to which such mouse models recapitulate the human phenomenon remains to be determined."

### 3.3 Balancing Conflicting Evidence

**Function**: Addressing the literature fairly.

*   "Although initial reports seemed contradictory, subsequent comparisons between the studies resolved that the discrepancy was due to age."
*   "Findings have been mixed; some studies have demonstrated a significant increase in biomarkers, whereas others have not identified any difference."
*   "It is likely that methodological differences account for these apparently discrepant findings."
*   "These discordant results might reflect the interindividual and genetic heterogeneity of the disease."

## 4. Presenting Quantitative Information

### 4.1 Describing Statistical Relationships

*   "At the group level, people with Alzheimer's disease can be differentiated from healthy control individuals based on biomarker levels with fair accuracy."
*   "The association between elevation of plasma p-tau and an increased risk of dementia could form the basis of a new diagnostic tool."
*   "Analysis of assays for p-tau181 on plasma samples revealed a noteworthy decrease in signal."

### 4.2 Conveying Magnitude of Effects

*   "Aged mice exhibited a 68% reduction in tumor number compared with young controls."
*   "Treatment induced a 2.3-fold increase in protein abundance."
*   "The group exhibited a 30% slowing in decline in cognitive measures."
*   "Adverse events occurred in less than 5% of participants in each group."

### 4.3 Reporting Findings (Results Section Conventions)

*   "Consistent with these findings, cytokine levels were found to be increased compared with controls."
*   "As expected, subjects with the mutation had a severe phenotype, whereas wild-type subjects were resistant."
*   "Unexpectedly, we found that the receptor could be readily detected on astrocytes."
*   "Notably, the effects of the inhibitor were consistent with those described above for the genetic deletion."

## 5. Describing Methodology & Approach

### 5.1 Rationale for Method Choice

*   "To identify novel regulatory mechanisms, we performed a genome-wide CRISPR–Cas9 screen."
*   "Given the central role of lipids in pathogenesis, targeting lipid metabolism has been considered a prime therapeutic approach."
*   "To mitigate potential confounding factors, we investigated the impact of age-matched controls."
*   "We therefore used a high-sensitivity method for chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing (iChIP)."

### 5.2 explaining Logical Flow of Experiments

*   "To test this hypothesis, we blocked the pathway in knockout cells."
*   "This information led us to hypothesize that the receptor might be involved."
*   "Encouraged by these findings, we next assessed whether this effect was conserved in humans."
*   "We next investigated the properties of the device, as mechanical stress is likely to be the main source of failure."

## 6. Synthesis & Implications (Discussion)

### 6.1 Interpreting Findings

*   "Our work introduces and characterizes an unforeseen role for 7-dehydrocholesterol in modulating ferroptosis."
*   "These findings suggest a shared pathophysiological mechanism involving inflammatory responses to pathology."
*   "This mechanism probably represents a major route of the system-driven influences on behavior."
*   "These observations provide valuable insights into the biology of the disease and the mode of action of risk genes."

### 6.2 Speculating on Mechanisms

*   "A plausible theory is that the two clinical presentations represented distinct subtypes."
*   "It is possible that some of the reported benefits of exercise may be linked at least in part to immune modulation."
*   "This would suggest that inflammation serves as a common mechanistic link between obesity and neurodegeneration."

### 6.3 Future Directions

*   "Future studies should address whether a more elaborate code of modifications influences chromatin accessibility."
*   "Addressing these remaining challenges will position blood biomarkers as an important tool in precision medicine."
*   "Moving forwards, it will be important to characterize the detailed mechanism through which peripheral signals are transmitted to the brain."
*   "Understanding the cellular and molecular underpinnings may promote the development of new therapeutic strategies."

## 7. ⚠️ Common Misuses to Avoid

**OVERUSED PHRASE**: "In this Review, we..."
*   **Problem**: Appears 15+ times in some papers, becomes formulaic.
*   **Better**: Vary sentence structures; use only 2-3 times maximum.
*   **Alternative patterns**:
    *   "Here, we examine..."
    *   "This Review explores..."
    *   "We focus on..." (no "In this Review" needed)

**BRACKET TRAP**: "Analysis of [method] on [samples] revealed [result]"
*   **Problem**: May tempt fabricating quantitative details.
*   **Better**: Only use this pattern when you HAVE the complete information from the original manuscript.
*   **Editing use**: Check author's data first, then enhance precision with appropriate vocabulary.

**CLAIM MISMATCH**: "These findings prove that X causes Y"
*   **Problem**: Correlational studies can't "prove" causation.
*   **Convention**: "These findings suggest that X influences Y" or "demonstrate correlation between X and Y".

**FALSE CERTAINTY**: "It is clear that..."
*   **Problem**: Rarely is anything in biology absolute.
*   **Convention**: "Current evidence indicates that..." or "It is generally accepted that..."
